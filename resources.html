    🎪 Gatherings & Festivals

    The US Ocarina Festival (Allen, TX)

    Each year, I organize and host the US Ocarina Festival in Allen, Texas. It all started in 2014 when a small group of online enthusiasts were wishing for a reason to gather. I promised to host an event if at least 10 people confirmed. Ten did, and we've been growing ever since!

    We have evolved from a local grocery store foyer into a televised concert hall hosting incredible performers from Norway, China, Japan, and across the United States.

    • Watch the History: The concert videos for 2014 and 2015 are available online courtesy of the Allen, TX television station.

    • Join Us This Year: We are expecting over 20 international guests from Japan alone. Check out the official Facebook Event Page for schedules and details!

    🛒 Full Ocarina Buying Guide

    ⚠️ Buyer Beware: Only purchase ocarinas—especially Zelda replicas—from trusted retailers. If a price seems too good to be true, it is likely a poorly tuned knockoff. Avoid retailers that sell cheap replicas, as they don't prioritize instrument quality. When in doubt, send me a quick message and my assistant or I will verify it for you.

    🗡️ Zelda Ocarinas

    Like many of you, Nintendo’s The Legend of Zelda franchise was my gateway to this instrument! While beautiful, many Zelda replicas feature a heavily rounded shape that can make ergonomics awkward for beginners. Here are the ones actually worth buying:

    • Premium Double: STL Zelda Double – If you want an instrument that functions beautifully without sacrificing ergonomics, this is my top pick. I’ve used it for numerous video recordings and my Princess Zelda Live concert series.

    • Best Value Single: STL Zelda Replica ($50) – The best possible value for a budget-conscious player. It sounds great, though the rounded shape does slightly impact the ergonomics (which is intentional to match the game design).

    • Great Alternative Shapes: Rotter Zelda Replica (excellent value if you are based in Europe) and 6th Street Ocarina. Both offer shapes that vary slightly from the game but provide much better physical ergonomics.

    • The Ultimate Luxury: Spencer Register Zelda Replica – Handcrafted works of art. They are expensive, but they play beautifully. If you are an experienced player, Spencer can even customize the playing characteristics to your exact preferences.

    • Avoid: Ocarinas that feature "in-game" fingering systems rather than standard 12-hole systems. These are novelties, not functional musical instruments.

    🧪 Plastic Ocarinas

    Beyond the Night by Noble, there are fantastic, durable plastic options across various tunings:

    • Alto/Tenor C: STL’s New Plastic AC (available with or without the Triforce logo), Osawa Focalink AC (requires less breath than the standard version), Focalink, Zin, Zin for Children (with keys), Noble, and Condor.

    • Soprano C:

      • STL: The loudest option.

      • Osawa-style Focalink: The quietest option with the sweetest tone.

      • Zin: The most balanced all-around performer.

    • Bass C: STL and Zin both produce good plastic options. STL’s version is more affordable and easier to source. Zin’s version is larger, slightly pricier, and quieter. (Note: I have tested the prototype for a upcoming Noble plastic bass, but cannot officially recommend it until the production model is released).

    • Multi-Chambered: The STL Zelda Plastic Double ($40) is a brilliant, low-cost way to try a multi-chambered instrument. It has a great tone and handles moisture better than the STL or Solist triples. While the Solist Tripleand STL/TNG Triples are solid entry-level practice instruments, I prefer the Solist.

    🪵 Wooden Ocarinas

    Wooden instruments are a financial investment, but their unique craftsmanship and warm tone are unmatched. My top three makers are:

    • North Country Workshop: They specialize exclusively in double-chambered polyphonic wooden ocarinas using the English pendant fingering system. Because there is no official method book for these, you will need private instruction. Myself, Cornell Kinderknecht, and Nancy Rumbel all offer private lessons for them (be sure to tell them I sent you!).

    • Hind Ocarina: Master maker Charlie Hind crafts incredible transverse and inline instruments. His transverse ocarinas require a unique, gentle, and perfectly even breath style. His higher-pitched models sound incredibly sweet, while his altos and tenors offer a beautiful, smoky texture. His unique Obelisk models are also highly recommended, though the thumb holes are swapped. (Note: I personally do not recommend his inline models, as they require an incredibly light breath style that falls outside my personal playing preferences).

    • Woodsound: Crafted by master maker Hyunil Ko. Every instrument is of impeccable quality, and his customer service is legendary (he even services second-hand instruments). He communicates wonderfully in English and can customize your instrument. Note that the ergonomics of his double ocarinas can be challenging depending on your hand size.

    🤝 Trusted Vendors & Makers

    I have personally bought from, performed with, and vetted every maker listed below. I am proud to call many of them friends who share a true love for expanding the world of the ocarina.

    • Oberon Ocarinas | Facebook Page

      • These are currently my absolute favorite US-made ceramic ocarinas. His transverse models don't come up for sale often and are a bit pricier, but they are worth every penny.

    • STL Ocarina | stlocarina.com

      • Our history goes back to 2009 when I won their first "Star Search" competition. They manufactured my custom Aria design, keeping it affordable without losing quality. I have worked with them as a consultant, local retailer, and video producer, and they have always fiercely supported my independence as an artist.

    • Songbird Ocarina | songbirdocarina.com

      • One of the oldest ocarina companies in the US (you might remember their ads in Nintendo Power!). They are the primary US distributor for Focalink and create gorgeous, uniquely glazed in-house designs. The owner kindly permitted me to perform one of his original compositions for my televised solo concert.

    • Takashi Ocarinas | pankuolong.url.tw

      • Made by my longtime friend Kuolong Pan. Before he ever started selling them commercially, he gifted me a handmade ocarina for my birthday. Getting to tour his workshop in Taiwan was a highlight of my Asia tour. He is widely considered one of the absolute best master makers in the world and offers custom tuning.

    • Night Ocarina

      • The "Night" behind the Night by Noble. I first tried their prototype at a post-festival dinner in South Korea and was blown away. Despite a chaotic Google-Translate barrier, we connected and became fast friends. They helped organize the Japanese legs of my 2013 and 2014 Asian concert tours. Their premium Fiorettoline is expensive but flawless.

    • Focalink

      • Run by Zack Shih, who is legendary for his customer service and instrument consistency. Zack was an incredibly generous host during the Taiwanese leg of my tour—even driving me around to visit his competitors' shops! I regularly perform using his instruments.

    • Imperial City Ocarinas (ICO)

      • Handcrafted by a single maker in China, Song Wei. While their earliest generations were a bit unpolished, their recent models are excellent, rustic, and highly affordable. I recommend buying brand new directly through ICO to ensure you get their latest generation and top-tier customer support.
