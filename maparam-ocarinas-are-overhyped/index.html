    🛒 Full Ocarina Buying Guide

    ⚠️ Buyer Beware: ONLY BUY OCARINAS FROM TRUSTED RETAILERS! If a price seems too good to be true (especially for Zelda replicas found on generic marketplaces), it is likely a badly tuned knockoff. Avoid retailers selling low-quality replicas; they don't care about your musical success. When in doubt, send me a quick message and my assistant or I will verify it for you.

    🗡️ Zelda Ocarinas

    Like many of you, I first discovered the ocarina through Nintendo’s The Legend of Zelda franchise! While beautiful, many Zelda replicas have a rounded shape that can make ergonomics awkward. Here are the ones that actually play beautifully:

    • STL Zelda Double ($40–$50+): If you want an instrument that matches non-decorated ocarinas in price and function, this is my top pick. I’ve recorded numerous videos with it and even used it for my Princess Zelda Liveconcert series.

    • STL Zelda Replica (Single Chamber, $50): The best value for a budget-conscious player who wants a single-chamber replica that looks the part and sounds great. The rounded shape affects ergonomics slightly, but it stays true to the game design.

    • Rotter Zelda & 6th Street Ocarina: Excellent alternatives that modify the appearance slightly to offer vastly improved ergonomics. Rotter is an especially great value if you are ordering from Europe.

    • Spencer's Zelda Replica: If money is no object, this is the gold standard. Spencer hand-crafts each one. They are expensive works of art that play flawlessly. Experienced players can even ask Spencer to customize the playing characteristics to their liking.

    • Avoid: Ocarinas featuring "in-game" fingering systems rather than traditional 12-hole systems. These are novelties, not musical instruments.

    🧪 Plastic Ocarinas

    Beyond the Night by Noble, there are fantastic, durable options across different keys:

    • Alto/Tenor C: STL’s New Plastic AC (available with or without the Triforce logo), Osawa Focalink AC (features a lower breath requirement than the standard plastic Focalink), regular Focalink, Noble, Condor, Zin, and the keyed Zin for children.

    • Soprano C: Zin, STL, and Focalink all make great versions. STL’s is the loudest, the Osawa-style Focalink is the quietest with the sweetest tone, and the Zin is the most balanced.

    • Bass C: STL and Zin both produce these. STL's version is more affordable and easier to source. Zin's version is larger, slightly pricier, and a bit quieter. (Note: Noble has a prototype bass in development, but I cannot formally recommend it until the production model launches).

    • Multi-chambered: The STL Zelda Plastic Double ($40) is an incredibly affordable way to try a multi-chambered instrument. It has great tone and handles condensation issues much better than the STL or Solist triples. While the Solist and STL/TNG triples are decent, durable budget entry points for practice or short performances, I wouldn't recommend them as a primary multi-chamber instrument.

    🪵 Wooden Ocarinas

    Wooden instruments are a premium investment, but nothing matches their warm, rich texture. I highly recommend these three master makers:

    • North Country Workshop: They specialize exclusively in double-chambered, polyphonic wooden ocarinas using the English pendant fingering system. Because there is no official method book for these, you'll need private instruction. Myself, Cornell Kinderknecht, and Nancy Rumbel all offer private lessons for them (tell them I sent you! 😊).

    • Hind Ocarina: Charlie Hind is a master craftsman. His transverse ocarinas are unique works of art that require a very soft, steady, and even breath across the entire range. His higher pitches are incredibly sweet, his altos/tenors have a gorgeous smokey texture, and his "Obelisk" models are highly recommended (note: the thumbholes are swapped, but easy to adjust to).

    • Woodsound: Hyunil Ko creates impeccable, world-class instruments and speaks English fluently for custom orders. While the ergonomics of his double-chambered ocarinas can be challenging depending on your hand size, his craftsmanship and customer service are legendary—he will even service his instruments if bought second-hand.

    🤝 Trusted Vendors & Friends

    I have personally purchased from, performed with, and verified the customer service of every maker below. I am proud to call many of them my friends and colleagues in the global ocarina community.

    Oberon Ocarinas

    These are currently my absolute favorite US-made transverse ocarinas. They are a bit pricier and sell out incredibly fast, so I recommend following his Facebook page closely to catch his batch releases.

    STL Ocarina

    My history with STL goes back to 2009 when I won their first "Star Search" competition. I love their instruments and respect their continuous drive to innovate. When I designed the Aria, I brought the design to them; they loved it so much they manufactured it, working closely with me to keep the price affordable for students without sacrificing quality. I've worked with them as a consultant, videographer, and local vendor. Truly wonderful people.

    Songbird Ocarina

    The official US distributor for Focalink, Songbird also crafts unique, beautifully glazed proprietary designs. As one of the oldest ocarina establishments in the US (you might remember them from Nintendo Power!), Mr. Songbird's artistic vision is unmatched. He was even kind enough to let me perform one of his original compositions for my televised solo concert.

    Takashi Ocarinas

    Crafted by my long-time friend Kuolong Pan in Taiwan. Long before he began selling them commercially, Kuolong gifted me one of his handmade ocarinas for my birthday. He is now widely regarded as one of the absolute best master makers in the world. His instruments beg to be played expressively. Getting to visit his workshop during my Taiwan tour remains a massive highlight for me.

    Night Ocarina

    The brilliant minds behind the "Night" half of the Night by Noble. I first tried their prototype at a post-festival dinner in South Korea and was so blown away that I used Google Translate to desperately get in touch with them. We became instant friends, and the president of Night went on to help organize the Japanese legs of my 2013 and 2014 Asian concert tours. Their premium ceramic Fioretto line is expensive but flawless.

    Focalink

    Managed by Zack Shih, who acts as a standard-bearer for incredible global customer service. Zack was an incredibly gracious host during my concert tour leg in Taiwan—even driving me around to visit his direct competitors' shops! I frequently perform using Focalink ocarinas and stand by them completely.

    Imperial City Ocarinas (ICO)

    In their earliest years, I wasn't a fan of their products due to low breath requirements and unpolished finishes. However, their recent models have vastly improved, and I now freely recommend them! Crafted by a single maker in China, Song Wei, they offer a beautiful, rustic appearance at a very affordable price point. I highly recommend buying them new directly through ICO to ensure you get their latest generation and top-tier customer service.
